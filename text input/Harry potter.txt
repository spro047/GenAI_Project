Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone begins the journey of Harry Potter, an orphan raised by his cruel relatives, the Dursleys. On his eleventh birthday, Harry discovers that he is a wizard and is invited to study at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. There, he befriends Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger, forming a close trio that becomes central to the story. Harry learns about the wizarding world, magic, and the mysterious death of his parents, Lily and James Potter. He also discovers that the dark wizard Lord Voldemort murdered his parents but failed to kill Harry, leaving him with a lightning-shaped scar. Throughout his first year, Harry uncovers a plot to steal the Philosopher’s Stone, an object capable of granting immortality, and prevents Voldemort from regaining power.

In Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets, Hogwarts is threatened by a hidden chamber opened by an unknown heir of Salazar Slytherin. Students are mysteriously petrified, and Harry learns that Voldemort once manipulated events through a magical diary belonging to his younger self, Tom Riddle. Harry defeats the monster inside the chamber, a basilisk, and destroys the diary, unknowingly eliminating one of Voldemort’s Horcruxes.

The story continues in Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban, where Harry discovers the truth about Sirius Black, who was falsely accused of betraying Harry’s parents. Sirius is revealed to be Harry’s godfather and an ally rather than an enemy. Harry also learns about betrayal by Peter Pettigrew, who secretly served Voldemort and contributed to his parents’ deaths.

In Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, Harry unwillingly participates in the dangerous Triwizard Tournament. During the final task, Voldemort fully returns to power using Harry’s blood in a dark ritual. Cedric Diggory is murdered, marking the beginning of open conflict between Voldemort and the wizarding world.

The rise of Voldemort intensifies in Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix. Harry and his friends form Dumbledore’s Army to resist the oppressive control of the Ministry of Magic and prepare for battle. Harry experiences visions connected to Voldemort’s mind and witnesses the death of his godfather Sirius Black during a battle at the Ministry.

In Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince, Professor Dumbledore teaches Harry about Voldemort’s past and the existence of Horcruxes, magical objects containing fragments of Voldemort’s soul. Harry learns that destroying all Horcruxes is necessary to defeat him permanently. The novel ends tragically when Severus Snape kills Dumbledore, though his true loyalty remains uncertain.

The final conflict occurs in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows. Harry, Ron, and Hermione leave Hogwarts to locate and destroy the remaining Horcruxes. They also uncover the legend of the Deathly Hallows, powerful magical artifacts linked to mastery over death. During the Battle of Hogwarts, many characters sacrifice themselves, including Snape, whose memories reveal that he secretly protected Harry out of love for Harry’s mother. Harry eventually learns that he himself contains a fragment of Voldemort’s soul. By willingly sacrificing himself, Harry destroys the final Horcrux within him. Voldemort is ultimately defeated when his own curse rebounds upon him, ending his reign of terror.

The Harry Potter series presents a complex network of relationships, organizations, magical objects, locations, and conflicts, making it highly suitable for knowledge graph construction. Key entities include characters such as Harry Potter, Voldemort, Hermione Granger, Ron Weasley, Albus Dumbledore, and Severus Snape; locations such as Hogwarts and the Ministry of Magic; and objects such as Horcruxes, the Philosopher’s Stone, and the Deathly Hallows. Relationships include friendship, mentorship, rivalry, family ties, alliances, and magical ownership, all interconnected through events spanning the seven novels.
